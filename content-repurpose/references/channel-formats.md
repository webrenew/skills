# Channel Formats

Limits change; check each platform's current help page when a post is rejected. The examples
repurpose one post: "Water heater leaking from the bottom? What it means" by Harbor Plumbing.

## Google Business Profile

- Text up to 1,500 characters. The first 2 lines show before "More": put the answer there.
- No phone numbers or email addresses in the text. Use the Call button.
- One photo, 4:3, at least 400 x 300 pixels (1200 x 900 works well), real work only.
- Button: Learn more (to the post), Book, Call now, or Order online.
- Offers: use Add offer with a title, start and end dates, terms and, if any, a code.

```
Water on the floor under your water heater? Check where it starts before you decide.
A leak from the tank itself means replacement. A leak from a valve is often a repair
from $149. Last month in Mount Pleasant, the rust was at the tank seam. Our post shows
how to find the source in 10 minutes.
[Photo: rusted tank seam] [Learn more → /blog/water-heater-leaking-from-bottom?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp&utm_content=water-heater-leaking-from-bottom]
```

## Facebook

- 2 to 4 short lines before the link. The link preview uses the post's cover image.
- A native photo post with the link in the text often reaches more people than a bare link.

```
"Is my water heater done?" We hear it every week.
The quick test: dry the floor, wait 10 minutes, and see where the first drop shows up.
Valve = usually a repair. Tank seam = time for a new one.
How to check, with photos from a real job: [link with UTM]
```

## Instagram

- Caption up to 2,200 characters; about 125 show before "more".
- Links in captions do not click. Use the link in bio or a story link sticker.
- Carousel up to 10 images, 4:5 (1080 x 1350) shows largest in the feed.
- Alt text on each image (Advanced settings).

```
Slide 1: "Water heater leaking from the bottom?" over the job photo
Slide 2: Where to look: valve, relief pipe, tank seam
Slide 3: The rusted seam from the Mount Pleasant job
Slide 4: Valve leak = repair from $149. Seam leak = replace.
Caption: Most bottom leaks come from one of three places. Here is how to tell which one
you have. Full guide: link in bio.
#CharlestonPlumber #WaterHeater
```

## Nextdoor

- Post from the business page. Neighbours respond to a helpful tip, not an ad.
- Name the neighbourhood only when the job was there and consent covers it.

```
Tip for older homes in Mount Pleasant: many 1990s water heaters are near the end of their
life. If you see water at the base, here is a 10-minute check to tell a cheap repair from a
replacement: [link with UTM]
- Sam, Harbor Plumbing
```

## LinkedIn

- From the owner's own profile; the business page can share it.
- A short story with a lesson, 3 to 6 short paragraphs. The link can go in the post.

## Short video (Reels, Shorts, TikTok)

- 30 to 60 seconds, vertical 9:16.
- The answer in the first 2 seconds, on screen as text.
- Real footage from the job, the owner's voice, captions on (many people watch muted).

```
0-2s   Text: "Water under your water heater?" (shot of wet floor)
2-15s  Sam points to the drain valve, relief pipe, tank seam
15-35s The rusted seam close-up: "When it's here, the tank is done."
35-45s "If it's the valve, that's usually a repair."
45-50s Text: "Full guide on our site" + business name
```

## Service-page FAQ

- Add the question and the post's answer block (40 to 60 words) to the hub service page's FAQ.
- Link to the post for the full answer. Use FAQ markup only if the page already shows
  visible Q&A with matching text.

## Email

- Only contacts with marketing consent. One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 headers, as in
  `launch-speed-to-lead`) and the postal address in the footer.
- Subject line is the question, under 50 characters. One link, with UTM.
- Plain and short: 80 to 150 words, from the owner by name.
