# AI Tells to Remove

Readers stop trusting a post when it reads like a machine wrote it. Remove each of these, then
read the post aloud in the owner's voice. Put words the owner dislikes in the kit's
`voice.wordsWeAvoid`. Do not copy this list into `neverSay` in the business profile: the lead
triage agent matches `neverSay` as a substring of every reply draft, and common words here
would send ordinary replies to manual review.

## Words and phrases

| Remove | Use instead |
|---|---|
| delve, dive into, deep dive | look at, or say the thing |
| navigate (a problem), landscape, realm, world of | the actual thing: "pick a water heater" |
| in today's fast-paced world, in this day and age | delete |
| whether you're a ... or a ... | name the one reader |
| it's important to note, it's worth noting, keep in mind | delete; state the fact |
| crucial, vital, essential, paramount | say why it matters, with the consequence |
| comprehensive guide, ultimate guide, everything you need to know | say what the post answers |
| unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, harness, leverage | the plain verb: get, use, make |
| seamless, robust, game-changer, cutting-edge, top-notch | a number or a named outcome |
| peace of mind (as filler) | what the reader no longer has to worry about |
| tailored, bespoke solutions | what the business does for this reader |
| look no further, we've got you covered | the call to action itself |
| in conclusion, to sum up, at the end of the day | a 2-line summary with no label |
| embark, journey, testament to, tapestry, beacon | delete |
| Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally (to start a sentence) | Also, or a new paragraph |

## Patterns

- **The rule of three, everywhere.** Lists of exactly three adjectives or benefits in every
  paragraph. Use the number of items the facts have.
- **"It's not X, it's Y."** One is enough per post, if any.
- **Questions the post asks itself:** "So, what does this mean for you?" Answer directly.
- **Every section the same shape.** Real posts vary: some sections are one paragraph, some a
  list, some a photo and a caption.
- **Empty openers:** a first paragraph that restates the title. The answer comes first.
- **Hedges stacked on hedges:** "may potentially help to some extent". State what is true, and
  where it stops being true.
- **Fake experience:** "As experts, we know..." with no job, number or story behind it. Use a
  proof bank entry or cut the line.
- **Em dashes and semicolons** in every paragraph. Use full stops.
- **Bold on random phrases.** Bold only the one thing a skimming reader must not miss.
- **Emoji in headings.** None.

## Check

After the edit, three tests:

1. Could a competitor publish this paragraph with only the name changed? Rewrite it with a
   proof bank entry, or cut it.
2. Would the owner say this sentence to a customer on the phone? If not, rewrite it in the
   kit's voice.
3. Does each sentence add a fact, a step or a reason? If not, cut it.
