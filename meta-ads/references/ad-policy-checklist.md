# Meta ad policy and honesty checklist

Run it on every new ad before it goes to the owner for approval. Meta's policies and the law
change; when an item is unclear, read Meta's current Advertising Standards and ask the owner,
who decides with their own adviser.

## Special ad categories

Declare the category at the campaign level if the ad offers or promotes any of these:

| Category | Examples for a small business |
|---|---|
| Housing | Rentals, a property manager's listings, home sales, mortgage or home insurance offers, home equity |
| Employment | "We're hiring", job fairs, apprenticeships, staffing |
| Financial products and services (credit) | Financing for a roof or HVAC, payment plans with credit, loans, credit cards, insurance in some regions |
| Social issues, elections or politics | Ads about a ballot measure, a public policy, or a candidate. These also need ad authorization and a "Paid for by" disclaimer |

What a declared category changes (check the current rules):

- No targeting by age, gender or ZIP code; a minimum location radius.
- Fewer detailed targeting options; no lookalike audiences.
- The ad still needs the same honest copy and a working landing page.

Running a hiring ad and a service ad? Use two campaigns: one declared, one not.

## Honest content

- [ ] Reviews and testimonials are real, from real customers, in their own words, with their
  permission. The source (Google review link, signed form, email) is on file.
- [ ] No review or testimonial written by staff, family, the agency or an AI as if it came
  from a customer. No paid or incentivized reviews shown as independent. (US FTC rule on
  consumer reviews and testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, in force since October 2024.)
- [ ] An endorser who was paid, got a discount, or is staff says so in the ad.
- [ ] Before and after photos are the same job, not edited to exaggerate, and the customer
  agreed to their use.
- [ ] Prices, "free", "same-day", ratings, years in business and license claims are true
  today and shown on the landing page.
- [ ] No fake urgency ("only 2 spots left" when it is not true), no fake countdown timers.
- [ ] AI-generated or heavily edited images of the business's work are not presented as real
  jobs. If an image is illustrative, the ad does not claim it is the business's work.

## Meta Advertising Standards (common rejections for small businesses)

- [ ] No personal attributes in copy: "Are you in debt?", "Struggling with your weight?",
  "Other homeowners in Summerville" with a personal hook. Talk about the service, not the
  person's traits.
- [ ] No before and after images for weight loss or cosmetic procedures where the policy
  limits them.
- [ ] Health, supplements, alcohol, dating, financial and some other services have extra
  rules or need permission.
- [ ] Text on images is short and readable on a phone.
- [ ] The landing page works, matches the ad, and has a privacy policy.

## Instant forms

- [ ] The privacy policy link points to the business's own policy.
- [ ] Custom disclaimers or consent checkboxes are there if the business sends marketing
  (`launch-lead-capture`, section 7).
- [ ] Higher intent form type, one qualifying question, a thank-you screen with the next step
  and the phone number.
- [ ] Leads sync to the lead store within minutes; a test lead arrived end to end.
